Unciphered — Privacy Policy

Effective with version 3.2.0 on iPhone, iPad and Android

Unciphered is an offline word game. It has no accounts, no sign-in and no social features. The app itself sends nothing about you to us; on iPhone and iPad it asks our website whether a newer version is out, and, if you allow it, tells Google how the game is played and which of our ads brought you to the game (below). Your progress can be kept in your own iCloud or Google backup, so it follows you to a new phone (below). The one exception is the Unciphered App Clip, the instant one-puzzle version that opens from a link, which reports anonymous play statistics. This policy describes both, and the Android app, which works the same way as the iOS app.

What the app itself collects

Nothing. Your progress — solved phrases, coins, hints, streak, settings — is stored in a file on your device. We operate no servers for it and cannot see your data. The one thing the app can send is the measurement described in the next two sections, and only if you allow it.

Measuring our ads

We advertise Unciphered on Google. To learn which of those ads work, the app includes one Google software kit, Google Analytics for Firebase, used only for this and for understanding how the game is played (the next section).

What is sent, while it is on: a random app ID created for this purpose; when the app was installed and opened, and how long it was in use; when you solve your first puzzle; when you buy something in the game (the item and price, as Apple reports them); and your device’s model, system version and language. Google sees your internet address when it receives this, as any website would, and uses it for an approximate location (country or city).

What is never sent: your name, email address or account, your Apple or Google advertising identifier (the app cannot read it and never asks to track you), or anything that lets Google show you personalised ads: that permission is always refused.

Your choice. In the UK, the European Economic Area and Switzerland, the app asks first, on its welcome screen, and sends nothing unless you say yes. Elsewhere it is on from the start. Anywhere, you can turn it off or on in Settings › Help improve Unciphered. Turning it off stops it at once and deletes the random app ID; if you never say yes, the Google kit is not even started.

Who receives it: Google, which uses it to measure our ad campaigns and keeps it for fourteen months, under Google’s privacy policy. We look at totals, such as how many installs an ad brought in; nothing we receive tells us who you are.

Understanding how the game is played

With the same switch, the app also tells us how the game is played, so we can see where puzzles are too hard or too easy, whether the tutorial helps, and which offers are worth showing.

What is sent, while it is on:

  • when the app is opened, how long it is in use, and which of its screens you visit;
  • the tutorial steps you complete or skip;
  • the puzzles you start, finish, fail or leave, named by our own puzzle number (for example en-0001), with rounded time and progress, and the number of hints, mistakes and lives;
  • coins earned and spent;
  • the offers you see or tap, and the purchases you start, cancel or complete;
  • whether reminders are on, and which reminder you tapped;
  • your streak, rounded into bands.

Each comes with the random app ID and your device’s model, system version and language.

What is never sent: your name or account, the letters you type, your answers, any text you write, or your advertising identifier.

Your choice: the same as for measuring our ads, and one switch covers both: Settings › Help improve Unciphered. In the UK, the European Economic Area and Switzerland nothing is sent until you say yes.

Who receives it: Google, as our processor for Google Analytics. It is kept for fourteen months, so we can compare one year with the next, and then deleted. We look at totals and trends; nothing we receive tells us who you are.

Checking for a newer version

On iPhone and iPad, the app checks unciphered.ghoroury.dev for a newer version; nothing about you is sent. It is a plain request for a small public file listing the latest version, with no account, identifier, progress or device details attached. Like any request on the internet, it reaches our hosting provider, Cloudflare, with your network address. If you are offline the check is skipped and the game plays as normal. On Android, update notices come from Google Play on your phone, not from us.

Your progress in iCloud and Google backup

If you’re signed in to iCloud, your Unciphered progress (puzzles solved, your Journal, streak, coins and settings) is stored in your own iCloud account so it can follow you to a new iPhone or iPad. We can’t see it, and you can remove it in iOS Settings → [your name] → iCloud → Manage Storage.

On Android, your progress is included in your phone’s Google backup, so it comes back when you reinstall or move to a new phone. We can’t see it, and you can turn it off in your phone’s backup settings.

Deleting the app deletes the copy on your device; a copy in your iCloud or Google backup stays until you remove it there.

The App Clip

The App Clip lets you play a single puzzle from a link without installing the app. To learn which puzzles make a good first game, and which links and ads bring players, it sends one anonymous event at each step of that puzzle: opened, solved, ran out of lives, left without solving, used a hint, or tapped “Get Unciphered”. Each event says:

  • which puzzle it was;
  • where the link came from, when the link says so (for example, which ad campaign);
  • for the end of a puzzle: how long it took, how many hints were used and how many lives were lost;
  • the App Clip’s version number.

No name, account, email, device identifier or advertising identifier is ever sent, and no location. Nothing sent can be connected to you or used to follow you across apps or websites, and we do not track you. Events are sent to our server at unciphered.ghoroury.dev, run on Cloudflare, which stores only the event itself (not your IP address) and deletes it automatically after a limited period. We use it only to improve the game and our ads; it is never sold or shared.

If you solve a puzzle in the App Clip and then install the app, the app picks up that puzzle as solved. This happens on your device, through storage the App Clip and the app share; nothing about it is sent anywhere.

Share links

You can share a puzzle with a link. Opening one of these links in the app sends nothing. If a link is opened in a web browser instead, the page at unciphered.ghoroury.dev counts the visit anonymously, as its own privacy page explains. No cookies are used.

Purchases

In-app purchases are processed entirely by Apple. We never see your payment details. The app keeps a local record of completed transaction identifiers solely so a purchase is never credited twice; that record stays on your device.

Advertising

Unciphered contains no advertising. Running out of tries offers an extra try — free, or for in-game coins earned by playing. Its only third-party software kit is Google Analytics for Firebase, for the measurement described above.

We advertise Unciphered elsewhere. When you arrive from one of those ads, the link names the campaign, and the App Clip includes that name in the events described above. That is how we learn which ads work; it involves no identifier and no tracking.

A future version may introduce an optional rewarded ad (watch an ad for an extra try, never required). If that happens, this policy will be updated first, and the app’s App Store privacy label will change with it.

Feedback and ratings

The app may, rarely, ask whether you are enjoying it. Choosing to rate opens Apple’s own App Store review prompt; choosing to send feedback opens a short Google Form in your browser. Both are optional, both leave the app, and the app records only that it asked. Anything you write in the form is handled by Google under its privacy policy and read by us.

Reminders

If you turn reminders on, the app can send you up to two notifications a day — one around midday when a new puzzle is ready, and one in the evening when that day’s secret level is still within reach. They are scheduled on your device by the app itself: nothing is sent to a server and no device token is created. We are not told whether a notification was delivered or ignored; if you allow the measurement above, the app notes that you opened it from a reminder, and which one. Reminders are off until you ask for them, and you can turn them off again in Settings or in iOS Settings at any time.

Android

Unciphered for Android works the same way. The app keeps your progress in one file on your device and sends nothing to us; your phone’s Google backup can include that file, as described above. From version 3.2.0 it measures our ads and how the game is played exactly as described above, with the same switch and the same choice. There is no App Clip on Android. Coin packs and chapter collections are sold through Google Play Billing and processed entirely by Google; we never see your payment details, and the app stores only the transaction identifiers of completed purchases so a purchase is never credited twice. Choosing to rate opens Google Play’s own in-app review prompt. Optional daily reminders are local notifications scheduled on your device and can be turned off in the app’s Settings or in Android Settings at any time. There are no ads, no accounts and no advertising identifier, and no third-party SDKs other than Google Analytics for Firebase and Google Play’s billing and review libraries.

Children

Unciphered is a general-audience app and is not directed at children under 13.
